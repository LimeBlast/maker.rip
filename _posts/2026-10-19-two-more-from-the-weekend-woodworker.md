_As part of my ongoing restoration of maker.rip, I'm writing up the projects from the old version of this site that never got a post of their own._

I've written about the [workbench](/2018/08/21/i-built-a-workbench/) and the [patio table](/2018/09/03/patio-table-build/) from Steve Ramsey's [Weekend Woodworker](https://theweekendwoodworker.com/) course, and reviewed the course itself for Hackspace Magazine. Two builds from it only ever made it onto the old site's portfolio page, so here they are.

## Harmony garden bench

![A slatted garden bench painted dark grey, standing on paving slabs in front of a fence](/assets/img/2026/10/harmony-garden-bench.jpg)

The second build from the course, this is conceived to be a thing of beauty from the outset, enabling the learning woodworker to realise that you can create things of beauty with even the most basic of tools and materials.

![The grey bench against a brick wall in the sun, with a ginger and white cat sprawled asleep underneath it](/assets/img/2026/10/harmony-garden-bench-cat.jpg)

Apricat approved.

## Sonoma Vineyard coffee table

![A pine coffee table with a framed top standing on a cream rug in front of a white fireplace](/assets/img/2026/10/sonoma-coffee-table.jpg)

The fourth build from the course, this beautifully designed coffee table is the first piece of furniture on it meant for indoors, and as such has a lot of attention to detail. I'd ordered the materials just before lockdown, so they arrived late, and I built it over the May 2020 bank holiday weekend. I spent far too long trying to get a true 45° on the mitre saw, and went off script on the top: the technique for seating the surface in its frame needs a dado blade, which you can't get in the UK, so I glued the frame to the edge of the top instead. Varnish finish, carefully stretched from the small amount I had left.

I'll be honest, I didn't think it would come out as nicely as it did, although there are some issues here and there. As long as I don't point them out to anyone, they're hidden enough that no one will notice.

![The coffee table from the doorway, between a leather sofa and the television](/assets/img/2026/10/sonoma-coffee-table-2.jpg)

It's ever so slightly too large for our living room, and the reclining sofas need room for their footrests, but we're making it work. Living room photos by Lucy.
